Reading · Learning · Financial education

Read, learn and
manage your money better

Quebec, Canada · 6 min read · Education · Books · Resources

"There's a difference between knowing something and truly applying it in your life. Sometimes, hearing the right idea at the right moment is enough to change everything."

I must admit that the advice I share can sometimes seem simple — even obvious to some people. These aren't revolutionary strategies or ultra-complex concepts. But over time, I've understood that there's a big difference between knowing something and actually applying it day to day.

Sometimes, an idea heard at the right moment simply acts as a reminder. A reminder of notions we already knew, but had set aside. And that's often how real changes begin — with a small moment of awareness.

Why financial education matters so much

The problem with finances is that when you don't understand a money-related topic, you tend to avoid it completely. You tell yourself it's too complicated, that you don't have the necessary knowledge, that you'll look into it later.

But years go by fast. And financial mistakes accumulate: bad habits, no savings, debt, fear of investing, poor tax management. At some point, you have to accept talking about it — and getting serious about it.

"All the knowledge I've acquired, I developed on my own. I'm neither a financial planner nor an advisor. I'm simply someone who loves to learn."

How I learn — my main sources

Personal finance books
The psychology of money, financial habits, long-term investing — I've always genuinely loved reading, and books remain my favorite source of learning.
Podcasts while working
I listen to podcasts on finance, investing and tax while I work in my business. By regularly consuming educational content, the concepts naturally integrate over time.
Online courses
Revenu Québec offers free training for self-employed workers. Cégep Beauce-Appalaches offers tax training for entrepreneurs. Accessible resources that prevent many costly mistakes.
Blogs and honest accounts
Reading accounts from people who speak honestly about their relationship with money — their mistakes, their successes, their reflections. Money remains a very sensitive topic in our society, and talking about it in a healthy way really helps.

The money I save, I reinvest

For people who aren't comfortable managing their finances alone, there are competent financial planners. Personally, it's not an avenue I use for now — simply because I love learning, understanding and managing on my own.

And honestly? Every dollar saved in fees or bad decisions, I prefer to reinvest. Because ultimately, every dollar saved today can become an asset that keeps growing through compound interest.

You don't retain everything you read. But sometimes, a single idea is enough to change a habit — and small repeated changes over many years can have an enormous impact on a person's financial stability.
