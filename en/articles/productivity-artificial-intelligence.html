Artificial Intelligence as a Productivity Lever: Learn, Equip, Act

Quebec, Canada · 9 min read · Productivity · AI
ⓘ Disclosure: This article contains affiliate links. If you sign up or purchase through my links, I earn a commission at no extra cost to you. My recommendations are based on my personal use.

When we talk about investing, we think of the stock market, real estate or entrepreneurship. Yet one of the best investments you can make today is in your own skills, and more precisely in your ability to use artificial intelligence.

Used well, AI does not replace your work: it multiplies what a single person can accomplish. And on the road to a million, this equation is clear. More productivity means more time and income; more income means more capital to invest.

This article brings together both sides of the question. First, where to learn AI seriously and for free. Then, which tools to use concretely, how, and at what price. The goal is for you to leave knowing exactly what to do, not just why to do it.

A word of honesty before we begin. This article draws on a testimonial: an entrepreneur says she multiplied her business efficiency by about seven by relying on AI. This "seven times" is an individual anecdote, not a guaranteed result. Results vary enormously from one person to another, and depend entirely on taking action. Keep this nuance in mind throughout your reading.

AI as a productivity multiplier

Artificial intelligence is already transforming the way we work. It saves time, automates repetitive tasks, improves the quality of your work and increases your productivity. It is no longer a technology reserved for programmers: entrepreneurs, freelancers, employees and even students can benefit from it.

The good news is that you do not need to spend thousands of dollars to start. Learning to use AI does not guarantee success, but ignoring this shift could leave you behind. Professionals who learn to integrate these tools intelligently into their daily work will often have a real edge in efficiency.

The right way to frame the question is not "which tool should I buy", but "which role is missing in my business today". An assistant who drops nothing? A writer who prepares your communications? A prospecting lead who finds you clients? That is how to view these tools: as roles you can now fill, without hiring.

The right mindset: rely on AI, but act

The most important message of the testimonial fits in one sentence: you must decide to rely on AI and see how it can help, but if you do not act on what it proposes, what is the point? AI surfaces opportunities; it is human action that creates the result. An agent can suggest ten leads in the morning, but none will produce anything until you have made it happen.

That is why the practical rule of this article is simple, and we will return to it in the conclusion: choose a single opportunity each day, and see it through. Not ten. One. Sustained over time, this discipline produces more than bursts of enthusiasm with no follow-through.

Where to learn AI for free

Before the tools, the basics. There is no need to pay to acquire AI skills: globally recognized organizations offer free, serious training. Here are four.

  • DeepLearning.AI — Founded by Andrew Ng, one of the most respected figures in the field, this platform offers free short courses on generative AI, prompt engineering and building agents. The non-technical course "AI for Everyone" can be taken for free in audit mode. Address: deeplearning.ai.
  • Google Skills — Formerly Google Cloud Skills Boost, Google's training platform offers structured paths on generative AI and cloud tools. Address: skills.google.
  • Microsoft Learn — Free paths covering the foundations of AI, with preparation for the recognized AI-900 certification. Address: learn.microsoft.com.
  • IBM SkillsBuild — Free training on the foundations of AI, with digital badges to add to your professional profile. Address: skillsbuild.org.

The classic trap is stacking courses without putting anything into practice. Choose a single platform, finish a path, apply it to a real task, then move to the next. Regularity beats intensity.

AI as a personal chief of staff

The most striking use case from the testimonial is a personal AI agent, which the entrepreneur named "Maximus", and which acts as a true chief of staff. Here is what such an assistant can do, concretely.

Email. It reads incoming messages, flags what arrives, and suggests how to reply. Reported result: missing nothing and becoming a far better responder. The agent can even draft and send emails on your behalf, presenting itself as your assistant, and attach a photo and a biography if needed.

The calendar. Connected to your calendar, it reminds you of your flights, the landing time, the hotel address and the things not to forget, because this information was added to the calendar at the time of booking.

Daily prospecting. Each day, it proposes ten concrete ways to connect with new clients in your city: local events not to miss, key people to approach, how to position yourself. The strong idea is there: AI brings opportunities to you, opportunities you would not have thought of.

This kind of assistant is built by giving it the right information: your context, your biography, your preferences. The more you inform it, the more useful it becomes.

Three immediate gains: email, marketing, prospecting

Three levers give a quick return and deserve to be activated right away.

Email. Process, sort, reply faster and better. It is often the first place you recover hours each week.

B2B marketing. Produce in a few hours what used to take weeks: research, messages, content for clients and prospects.

Prospecting. AI-assisted research to find where your clients are and how to reach them.

The honest framing stays the same: these levers increase efficiency, but the size of the gain depends on you and your consistency.

The toolbox

Base44 — build agents, apps and websites

What it does: build AI agents (the "super agents") that talk to you where you already communicate — WhatsApp, Telegram or iMessage — and connect to your tools, such as Gmail. It also lets you build mobile apps (to publish on the App Store or save directly to the phone as a web app) and create websites.

How to use it: you describe to the agent what you want it to do, then you connect it to the tools it needs to access. Important security tip: always test on your personal Gmail first before connecting anything important, and review the security settings.

ChatGPT (OpenAI) — images and writing

What it does: image creation, currently of very good quality, and writing.

How to use it: generate visuals and text by describing precisely the style you want.

Manus — presentations and slides

What it does: create presentations and slides. It is the testimonial's favorite tool for this task.

How to use it: specify precisely the style you want, then, and this is the little trick from the testimonial, ask it to create the presentation as if you had paid $20,000 for it. This simple parameter radically changes the output. The whiteboard style is cited as particularly effective.

Claude (Anthropic) — writing, Claude Code, Claude Cowork, Chrome extension

What it does: content writing and, once Claude Code and Claude Cowork are mastered, much more: producing websites, analyses and documents in a single workflow.

You ask it to examine it like a usability tester; it actually clicks every button, browses the site, then reports the problems: broken navigation, non-functional button, colors to review. You watch it work and you get a report.

What AI will not do for you

For the sake of honesty, it must be said clearly: AI replaces neither your judgment nor your work. It will not define your strategy for you, will not close your sales, and will not supervise its own results. It makes mistakes you must know how to spot and correct. It requires being framed, verified, and learned. The productivity gain is real, but it belongs to whoever stays at the controls. AI is a lever, not an autopilot.

Getting started safely

The prudent approach comes down to a few principles. Start small. Test first on a personal account, for example your personal Gmail, before connecting sensitive tools. Check the security settings of each service. Grant access only to what is strictly necessary. And if your work involves client data, remember that its confidentiality must be protected at all times; never entrust sensitive information to a tool without having weighed the consequences.

One opportunity a day, and the cumulative effect

Let us return to the practical rule. AI can bring you about ten opportunities a day; you choose only one, and you make it happen. Repeated each day, this discipline compounds over time, exactly according to the logic of the road to a million: small, constant actions that accumulate and end up changing everything.

And this is where the loop closes. The time and money AI saves you must not dissolve into daily life. They are fuel. Reinvested in your income, then in your investments, they become the very engine of your financial independence. Productivity is not an end: it is the first link in a chain that leads to investing.
