How to save
$10,000?

Quebec, Canada · 8 min read · Savings · Income · Side hustle

"I don't believe in tightening your belt more. I believe in earning more money — and understanding how to keep it."

I know that saving $10,000 can seem very difficult with today's cost of living. And honestly? I used to be a big spender. As soon as money came in, it was gone — often on things I didn't really need. What changed everything for me were a few simple habits I developed over time.

Tips that actually worked for me

Pay yourself first
As soon as money comes in, it goes directly into my investment account — before I even have a chance to spend it. Mentally, when money is separate, you think twice before touching it.
Buy less, not nothing
I realized I was often buying for the pleasure of buying — and not even opening all the packages. Now, before buying something, I ask myself if I'll really need it once I have it.
Sell what you don't use
Poshmark, Facebook Marketplace — anything sitting at home can become cash. Even if I sell for less than I paid, the interest on that invested money will make up the difference long term.
Go earn more income
Rather than depriving myself further, I prefer to increase what comes in. A service to offer, a skill to develop, a side hustle — it's often more effective than cutting into your lifestyle.

"Nobody is coming to save me. If it's not me, it's nobody. That mindset pushed me to always find a way."

The real strategy — increasing your income

Cutting back has its limits. You can only cut so much. But the potential to increase your income is almost unlimited.

My approach: identify a skill or service to offer. Someone who cooks well can do catering. Someone handy can offer painting or renovation. Someone comfortable with technology can build websites or offer IT support.

What if you don't yet have a skill to offer? That's where training becomes an investment. In Quebec, there are AEC, DEP and ASP programs that train you in a few months for well-paying trades. Some lead directly to entrepreneurship.

My personal advice

Look for all trades that require a professional order or recognized license. When you have an official certification, you can offer your services credibly, charge your fair value, and build a client base. The financial planner, the electrician, the accountant, the nurse — all have a professional order. And all can work independently.

The real message

I'm not saying it's easy. But I've always believed that nobody is coming to save us — and that it's precisely that reality that pushes us to find creative solutions.

A side hustle can start small and become your main source of income. That happened to me. And sometimes, working on your own project — even modestly — gives a quality of life and freedom that no employer can offer.
