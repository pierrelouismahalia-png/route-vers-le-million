Credit score

Check your score for free. In Canada, there is no longer any reason to pay to know your score, or to stay in the dark.

  • Borrowell* gives you free access to your score and your Equifax file, with regular updates.
  • Credit Karma does the equivalent for TransUnion. No affiliate link here, deliberately — I recommend it anyway, because checking both bureaus once a year is what lets you spot errors on file.

See my score for free* →

An important reminder: checking your own score is a soft inquiry — it has no impact whatsoever on your rating. It is the costliest myth in personal finance.

Books that changed how I see money

Three books, three complementary angles: why accumulate, how the people who actually get there think, and the mindset for building from nothing.

Die With Zero, by Bill Perkins* — the book that made me rethink the very purpose of accumulating. Save, yes, but for what, and for when? A read that unsettles you in the right place.

See on Amazon* →

Related article: my article on Die With Zero

The Millionaire Next Door, by Thomas J. Stanley and William D. Danko* — the classic study of real American millionaires: not the ones on social media, the ones next door, frugal, discreet, consistent. The data-driven demonstration that wealth is built exactly as this blog describes.

See on Amazon* →

Start With Yourself, by Emma Grede* — from the co-founder of Good American and founding partner of Skims: an East London school-leaver who became one of the most accomplished businesswomen of her generation. A book about mindset, about dismantling the inherited old ideas about money, ambition and work. The one I recommend to the entrepreneurs who read me.

See on Amazon* →
